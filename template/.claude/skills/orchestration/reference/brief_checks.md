# Brief checks before launch

Read before building a brief (`orchestration` §0 Governing skills and Pre-Dispatch Checklist point here).

## Governing skills

Before building a brief, load each skill whose workflow the job uses in this session's current context; repeat after compaction unless the client reattached the whole skill and that copy still matches the skill's `SKILL.md` on disk. Keep its source paths in the brief. A skill whose files the job only reviews or reads as data governs nothing, except that a job designing or judging what agent-facing text should hold is governed by the skill that owns that text's rules, though it edits nothing.

## Gather, then judge

Gathering and judging are separate jobs. Gathering evidence (search, extraction, counting, quoting a source) runs on the cheapest ladder row that does it reliably, because a quote check scores it. Its result reaches the judge, picked for the judgment's depth, through the engine's hand-off, with no hop through the parent. When the gatherer runs as a sidecar, the fan-out writes each job's final message to its `<label>.md` (a read-only job has no Write tool and never spills), and a gather command that closes its lenses into a checked dossier names that file in its result: launch the judge with that path once the gather ends. The judge's brief carries the evidence, the owner's words and the question, never the parent's conclusion or a pick it has not grounded, because a brief that hands over a conclusion gets it confirmed back. A top row at top effort is for open-ended judgment, never for gathering, because it prices a read a cheaper row does as well. A design gets its own job when it needs evidence you lack or judgment deeper than a ruling over evidence already in your context; a small design question stays with you, as a drive's sizing allows. You keep the orchestrator's calls: what runs, and ruling a job's findings against evidence.

## Brief checks

- The brief marks each premise observed, with its evidence (a path, or a command and its output), or suspected. A suspected cause goes to the delegate as a question to settle, never as the brief's premise.
- A brief for an investigation or fix lane embeds the project debugging skill's Recurrence Law rules 1–3 as text, because its delegate may run on a client that loads no skills; a lane returning a fix without its discriminating evidence is rejected at review.
- An executor's done-condition runs its dispatcher's checks of the returned work (its edited units' proof files, `dispatch_result.py check`, its live checks), so a defect is fixed inside the dispatch on the cheaper model. It also names the done criteria of the rules the write set falls under (each `rules/*.md` whose `paths:` match those files): read them before writing the line, because the executor's client may load none of them and its return is judged against them (`integration.md` §When it returns). Its runs cover the units its change touches: for tests, the slice rows `adaptation.json` `gate_cadence` names plus each test that references a member whose caller contract the change alters (what it frees, owns, returns, raises or requires), because a broken caller shows in a test outside the edited unit's own file; for renders or captures, the states it changed. No brief hands it a run over the whole product, as its proof or as its done-condition: the gate, a whole spec file, a second test project, a render or capture of every state, or any other run sized by the product rather than the change. The dispatcher runs each of those once at its merge or close (`rules/harness_tooling.md`, `change_control` §Gate cadence).
- A brief whose result feeds an owner question asks for each fact and each pick's basis in a `claims` fence (`reference/evidence_contract.md`), so the run verifies them before the ask, per `CLAUDE.interactive.md` §Answering and asking the owner. Decision rows with free-text facts return unchecked.
- A job whose result is a design or a judgment, not the execution of one (architecting, planning, design, debugging, judging another job's work) meets `orchestration` §4 *A brief leaves the thinking to the job*. Read the written brief against it: it carries the goal, the owners' words verbatim, the evidence paths and measurements, and what is fixed (their picks, locked parts, hard limits); each scope line, sub-question and deliverable heading traces to the owners' principle, never to a candidate, example or sub-question the briefer already held; and every seat of one question carries the whole question. A brief that fails is rewritten before launch. Its job finds the cause and the best answer, which can differ from what the owners' words name.
- The brief sets no limit its delegate stops at: no minutes, turn count or "stop after". A duration worry is the orchestrator's check-in, read from the job's progress (`reference/sidecar_runs.md` §Detached runs); a run that hits a cap discards every turn it paid for. Nor does it order the delegate to wait until a long job ends: a delegate waits out the runs it started, which end with its seat, and hands back only a job under its own launcher and record (`guards/common.md`).
- Text a delegate acts on orders a Read of a scratch file, never an existence check: Glob and Grep hide gitignored paths.
- The brief's paths resolve: `python3 .claude/tools/verify_doc_citations.py --kinds path --only missing <brief>` lists the misses, and each is fixed or confirmed as a non-path (a branch name) or a path the job creates. A path to the job's own report is the one the engine writes, `<spillDir>/<label>.md`.
- Run each check command the brief hands the job on the current tree before launch, because a check that cannot pass sends the job to debug the brief instead of its work. A check of the new behavior fails for the reason the job removes; any other check passes. Any other result (a missing path the job does not create, a crash, a red the job does not own) is fixed in the brief first.

## Return checklist

A job over enumerable items returns one checklist line per item, done with evidence or not done with its blocker; an unchecked item is not done. Read the checklist first, then spot-check its evidence; it never replaces a diff review or verification gate. `couldNotSatisfy` names real blockers, and a delegate reports a rail/brief conflict instead of resolving it silently.
