# Brief the defect class and the search obligation, not the candidates you happened to find

After fixing one temporal-coupling protocol, the parent dispatched an opus fixer. The brief's Secondary section read *"Audit for other instances and report. Unexamined candidates: <eight paths>"* — the output of the parent's own quick sweep.

The owner rejected the shape: *"the fixer shouldn't just be finding based on your 8 candidates the whole point is that you didn't really explore well enough or thorough enough to make me confident. That's why I told you to dispatch a new agent that can dedicate its entire process to finding and resolving those issues. So don't underscope an agent based on your incomplete research ever again."*

**Why it was wrong, beyond taste:** the delegate was dispatched BECAUSE the parent's exploration was thin. Handing it that exploration's output makes the thin sweep the delegate's ceiling — it can only confirm or subtract, never exceed. The parent pays full executor price for coverage it already had, and the resulting "audited, N found" reads as exhaustive when it is a restatement.

**The accepted shape:** scope by the defect class plus the discovery obligation. Name the smell, the searches to run, and the coverage report required; pass any known instances as *"already seen — not the boundary"*.

**How to apply:**
- Before sending a brief, ask what the delegate is allowed to find that you did not. No answer → the scope is a restatement, not a delegation.
- `orchestration` §11 (*"if you cannot enumerate the files, scope it further before dispatching"*) sizes a CONVERGED execution job, where the finding ledger IS the spec. An audit is the opposite case: not being able to enumerate is the reason it exists. Do not apply §11 to a discovery job.
- A dispatch whose value is coverage requires a coverage report — which searches ran, over which roots — not just a findings list. A findings list alone cannot distinguish a clean tree from a narrow sweep.
