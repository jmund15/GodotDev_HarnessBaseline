When user pushback arrives (especially with frustration), resist the reflex to abandon the whole approach. Restate the objection as a specific falsifiable claim and confirm it maps to a real defect in the current design — not to a mis-description of the mechanism — BEFORE any large pivot.

**Why:** A vaguely-scoped objection read as total rejection triggers an expensive, wrong pivot. Evidence (P3 node-lighting): the user's objection was "don't CONFLATE per-room lighting with visual disclosure" — a warning about coupling two systems' *outputs*. I misread it as "the global-follows-current lighting MODEL is wrong," pivoted to a per-space model + a full arch-doc rework, then had to revert all of it. The user's own words — "I didn't say per-room lighting couldn't apply to the whole screen — if anything that makes sense" — already ruled out the interpretation I acted on.

**How to apply:** A "you're conflating X and Y" / "you're describing it wrong" complaint is a *separation/clarity* problem — addressable by adding an invariant, a rename, or a boundary note — NOT a signal to redesign the mechanism. Diagnose the scope of the objection first: does it reject the mechanism, or the way I described/coupled it? Only the former warrants a pivot.
